    Worth knowing

    • You need the Vietnam e-visa to leave Noi Bai airport. There's no visa-free option for Indian passports. US$25 at evisa.gov.vn, roughly three working days. Without it you spend all 18 hours airside.
    • Philippine immigration will ask for paperwork. Indians get 14 days visa-free, but you must show a passport valid six months out, hotel bookings for every night, proof of funds and your Manila–Chennai ticket. Have them printed.
    • Add checked baggage when you book the internal flights. Cebu Pacific and AirAsia sell hand-carry-only fares. Twenty kilos costs ₱500–800 online and upwards of ₱2,000 at the counter.
    • Draw pesos before Palawan. ATMs in Coron and El Nido run dry regularly and almost every boat operator is cash-only. Carry USD to change rather than rupees — Bengaluru rates for pesos are poor.
    • Keep small bills. Nobody at a sari-sari store or on a jeepney can break ₱1,000. Split your withdrawals into hundreds early.
    • At the airports, take the white metered taxis or Grab. The yellow airport cabs at Mactan and NAIA run a higher meter for the same road.
    • Watch the season. December to May is the dry amihan; June to November is typhoon season, when Palawan boat tours get cancelled outright. Whatever the month, keep the morning of day 7 free of anything you can't miss.
    • Reef rules are enforced. No touching turtles, no standing on coral, reef-safe sunscreen at the sanctuaries — rangers fine on the spot.
    • Four of your seven days involve moving. Bus, two ferries and two flights. If anything slips, drop Bohol rather than Palawan — the ferry is the easiest thing to cancel.
    • Book the OceanJet Cebu–Bohol crossing ahead. Morning sailings fill up, and the pier wants you there 45 minutes early.
    • Emergencies: 911 works across the Philippines; in Vietnam it's 113 for police and 115 for an ambulance. Save the Indian Embassy in Manila's number before you fly.
    • Four Cebuano words carry you far: salamat — thanks; pila? — how much; lugar lang — stopping the jeepney; lami — delicious.
